\section{Purpose and operating principles}

The book has crossed from research project to operating asset. This manual is
the standing schedule for running it: what is checked, at what cadence, by
whom, against what number, and what may --- and may not --- be changed in
response. Three principles govern everything below.

\textbf{Cadence matches statistical power.} The book makes $\approx$599
verified fills a year --- about 2.4 per session, roughly five per name per
month. Daily returns are noise; monthly per-name returns are noise; a
\emph{quarter} is the first horizon at which a per-name verdict means
anything. The schedule is built so that no one is ever staring at a number
that cannot support a decision.

\textbf{The biggest risk to this book is a well-intentioned reaction.}
Sixteen tested proposals have died on the standard protocol; the survivors
are three wired vetoes and one manual pause. History says the harvest is
robust and the operator's improvements are usually not. Nothing in daily or
weekly operation authorises a change to prices, parameters or rules ---
change happens only through \S9.

\textbf{Execution truth lives in the blotter.} The models predict; the
blotter records what actually happened. Reconciling the two is not
bookkeeping --- it is how the gap-exit correction ($\sim$20pp/yr of
already-earned money) was found, and the weekly reconciliation exists to
find the next one.

\section{Daily runbook (market days, $\approx$10 minutes, David)}

\begin{checklist}
\item Script 1 ran; Query/Feed dates advanced; no \texttt{\#N/A} in the order block.
\item Pre-market column P populated (once Script 1 is upgraded); Gate
      Variables intact (M6 $=$ 0.04, M7 $=$ 0, M8 $=$ 4).
\item Breadth count (Alloc G43) plausible against the traffic lights in col N.
\item Script 2 ran; order count and prices at IBKR match the order block;
      zero-quantity BUY rows skipped; MU bids withheld if the pause window is
      active.
\item End of day: fills entered in the blotter; any manual action recorded in
      the discretionary log with one line of reason.
\end{checklist}

That is the whole daily job. \textbf{Do not review returns daily.} The only
daily triggers for action are the alarms in \S8.

\section{The Sunday package (weekly, 2--3 hours, interns)}

Markets are closed on Sunday: nothing in this package can touch a live order,
which is what makes it delegable. Standing order of work:

\begin{checklist}
\item \textbf{Reconcile} (45--60 min): IBKR activity statement vs the
      blotter --- every fill, fee, dividend and cash movement. Discrepancies
      are logged, not fixed silently; unexplained items escalate (\S8).
\item \textbf{The weekly one-pager} (10 min, tooled): run
      \texttt{python weekly\_onepager.py <workbook>.xlsx} against the saved
      workbook --- it writes a one-page HTML report beside it. Contents: book
      equity vs the plan line (Performance-tab assumptions); fills, exits and
      stops this week by name; DMA breadth count for each day of the week
      with gate-armed days marked; ATH proximity (cap territory); MU pause
      status (from the next-earnings date in Active Trading G11 --- keep it
      filled); open positions and their ages against the 50-day stop; a hold
      tracker; and data-quality checks (Query freshness, missing closes,
      blotter integrity). The intern's job is \emph{reading} it --- losing
      exits and stop-due flags are the items that escalate. PM vetoes are not
      on it: the sheet keeps no 9:00 history, so those are checked live on
      the morning run.
\item \textbf{Performance-tab check} (5 min): confirm the week's row filled
      (week ending, actual P\&L from AT col P --- blotter plus any closed
      discretionary trades) and that the cumulative-actual line updated on the
      chart. This is a \emph{recording} check, not a judgement: a single
      week's gap to the target line is noise by construction. The tab's
      verdicts are read monthly (\S5).
\item \textbf{Archive the data} (20 min): export the week's 5-minute bars
      (regular hours \emph{and} pre-market) for all book and watch-list
      names into the standard folders
      (\texttt{data\_5min/TICKER\_5min.xlsx},
      \texttt{data\_pm/TICKER\_pm.xlsx}). The archive is why the 2022 replay
      and the pre-market rule were possible; it only exists if it is kept.
\item \textbf{Watch-list update} (20 min): run the frozen watch-list vectors
      over the new week's data; append would-be fills to each candidate's
      paper track (\S7).
\item \textbf{Coming week} (10 min): earnings-calendar sync run (or check
      the Friday automation did); confirm whether an MU pause window opens.
\end{checklist}

If Sunday hours are not available, items 2 and 4 are scripted and the
package compresses to under an hour: reconcile, glance at the generated
one-pager, archive.

\section{Monthly review (first Sunday of the month, $+$1 hour)}

\begin{checklist}
\item \textbf{Book vs plan --- the Performance tab}: the standing instrument
      is the workbook's Performance sheet (between Notes and Allocation):
      weekly actual P\&L against a target compounding at 66\%/yr (the re-cast
      plan, set in C5 on 2 Sep 2026) from \$6{,}150{,}000 starting capital
      (assumptions editable in C4/C5), with
      cumulative lines charted. Read the \emph{cumulative} gap
      (column G / the chart), never a single week; the weekly increments are
      noise by construction. Judge the gap only against pre-agreed tolerance
      bands derived from the verified backtest's own dispersion --- inside
      the bands, \emph{no action and no narrative}. (Bands to be computed
      and appended to this manual as Appendix A.) Changing the target
      assumptions is a quarterly-review decision, not a monthly one.
\item \textbf{Activity pace}: fills and stops vs the $\approx$599/yr and
      $\approx$20/yr references. A collapse in fill count is a structural
      alarm even when returns look fine --- it means the bids stopped being
      touched.
\item \textbf{Execution audit}: the month's live fills vs engine-predicted
      fills (prices and dates). Persistent one-sided gaps are exactly how
      the gap-exit money was found; the next discrepancy of that class is
      worth more than any overlay.
\item \textbf{Interest and cash}: idle-cash interest credited vs expected;
      discretionary carve-out netting still correct if the flag was used.
\end{checklist}

\section{The quarterly review (Sep / Dec / Mar / Jun, half a day)}

The only forum with authority over composition. Agenda:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em, itemsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{Per-name verdicts.} Each name's trailing two quarters
      (annualised, verified from the blotter) against its planning anchor
      and its \emph{observed range} (the span of its train/test-half
      returns --- the honest ``any year lands somewhere in here''). A name
      is flagged only when persistently below \emph{both} its plan and the
      bottom of its range.
\item \textbf{Composition.} Flagged names join the funding-source list (VST
      has carried that flag since August). Removals and additions go through
      the marginal book test --- a name must out-earn or de-risk the book it
      joins --- never through sentiment. Additions come only from the watch
      list (\S7).
\item \textbf{Scheduled re-tests.} Standing diary: \textbf{AVGO}
      mid-September 2026 --- now an \emph{in-book} check, not an admission
      re-test (AVGO replaced RKLB on 2 Sep 2026): after the $\approx$4~Sep
      report, confirm the frozen AVGO vector's post-print behaviour and the
      pause question with a tenth report window in the data; \textbf{NEM}
      end-Q1 2027 (admits if reclassified uncorrelated or the plan clears
      50\%).
\item \textbf{Rules integrity.} Cell-diff the live workbook against the
      reference copy: gates wired, script-read ranges unmoved, Gate
      Variables correct. Any drift is restored from reference, not patched.
\item \textbf{Research queue.} New ideas proposed this quarter are listed
      with their information source and their nearest dead relative from the
      rejection ledger, then prioritised. Testing happens outside the review
      through the standard protocol.
\end{enumerate}

\section{The watch-list protocol}

The admission problem is that fresh parameter fits do not transport ---
backtest evidence for a new name is always the weakest evidence. The watch
list converts \emph{time} into the evidence money can trust:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em, itemsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{Admission to the list} (any time): candidate passes G0--G2 ---
      clean verifiable data, classification (AI-linked face the 50\% planning
      gate, uncorrelated 30\%), and an honest half-sample fit that clears its
      gate bar on the backtest.
\item \textbf{Freeze.} The reference vector is frozen on admission day and
      recorded. It is never re-fitted while the name is on the list --- the
      entire value of the list is that every subsequent week is genuinely
      out of sample.
\item \textbf{Paper-track.} Each Sunday the frozen vector runs over the
      week's archived data; would-be fills, exits and stops accumulate in
      the candidate's track record.
\item \textbf{Graduation} (quarterly review only): \emph{forward} paper
      performance clears the name's gate bar over at least two quarters,
      \emph{and} the marginal book test passes with the live rules on both
      sides. Both or neither.
\item \textbf{Capacity.} The list holds at most five names (each one costs
      weekly archiving). Current bench: \textbf{NEM} (watch-listed Aug 2026)
      --- AVGO left the list into the book on 2 Sep 2026, and RKLB left the
      book without joining the list (removed on risk grounds, not
      performance; re-admission would need a fresh case through G0--G5). A
      screening round to fill the remaining seats feeds the Q4 review.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Alarms and escalation}

\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=bcred!6, colframe=bcred, boxrule=0.8pt,
  title={\bfseries Act today (David, before next open)}, coltitle=white]
A script failed and orders are unplaced or unknown $\cdot$ a fill exists
with no corresponding order (or vice versa) $\cdot$ workbook gate cells
show errors or a script-read range moved $\cdot$ IBKR cash does not
reconcile to the blotter. Response: stop placing new orders until the state
is understood; resting exits stay working (they protect positions). Fix the
plumbing, never the strategy, under time pressure.
\end{tcolorbox}

\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=bcnavy!5, colframe=bcnavy, boxrule=0.8pt,
  title={\bfseries Note and wait for the scheduled review}, coltitle=white]
A losing week or month inside the bands $\cdot$ a name gated by the breadth
rule $\cdot$ a cluster of time-stops in a falling tape $\cdot$ an April-style
drawdown. The 2022 replay and the April 2025 episode are the evidence that
the wired rules handle these without help. The one discretionary instrument
that exists is the carve-out (Alloc E5/E6) --- deliberate, logged, and
outside the model's capital. There is no ``manual de-risking'' inside the
model's sleeves.
\end{tcolorbox}

\section{Change control}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.6em, itemsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{Workbook edits}: only against a saved reference copy, verified
      by full-workbook cell-diff (the wiring scripts are re-runnable);
      script-read ranges (\texttt{A18:J36}, \texttt{IBKR\_Orders}, blotter)
      never move.
\item \textbf{Strategy changes}: every proposal --- however small --- goes
      through the standard protocol: verified fills, train-half choice,
      frozen tested half, adopted only on a both-halves win (protection
      rules may instead win on drawdown dominance at $\approx$zero return
      cost, the breadth-gate standard). Vetoes may cancel an order, never
      re-price it.
\item \textbf{Parameters}: frozen. Re-fitting is not a maintenance activity
      --- it failed 1/18, 6/40 and every fresh-protocol comparison since.
      A specification \emph{bug} (the OU-sigma class) is fixed on
      discovery; a fit is never chased.
\item \textbf{Records}: every adopted change lands in HANDOVER and the
      relevant paper the same day, with the reproduction script in the
      repository.
\end{itemize}
