\section{The mandate}

Develop the watch list to its full strength ($\leq 5$ names), \textbf{freeze}
each candidate's analysis at admission to the list, and paper-track the frozen
work forward over the coming three months. The December quarterly review reads
your register and decides; graduation into the live book is a review decision,
never an analyst one, and the standing bar (\S3) asks for \emph{two} quarters
of forward evidence --- so December is your first formal checkpoint, and the
earliest realistic graduation is the Q1 2027 review unless the principal
decides otherwise on the December evidence. Your product is not a
recommendation to buy anything; it is a candidate whose \emph{forward} record,
earned after the analysis stopped, clears a pre-agreed bar.

\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=bcgold!8, colframe=bcgold, boxrule=0.8pt,
  title={\bfseries The one law}, coltitle=bcnavy]
\textbf{Freeze means freeze.} The reference vector fitted at admission to the
list is never touched again while the name is on it --- not re-fitted,
not ``improved,'' not nudged after a bad month. Three independent instruments
(forty historical folds; a fresh two-variant optimisation on verified fills; a
likelihood test of the price process) agree that parameter search does not
transport out of sample. The entire value of the watch list is that every week
after the freeze is \emph{genuinely} out of sample --- it converts time into
the only evidence money can trust. A re-fit resets the clock to zero and
quietly poisons the record.
\end{tcolorbox}

\section{What a candidate is being tested for}

The book trades two limit-order sleeves per name (a Bayesian trend filter and
an Ornstein--Uhlenbeck reversion model), buying morning weakness at
model-derived bids and selling at a fixed premium, with a 50-day time stop and
a pooled morning allocation across all names. What pays the premium is
\textbf{daily range} --- the candidate screen is, above all, a screen for
names volatile enough to pay for patience, liquid enough to fill at the bid,
and clean enough to verify.

\section{The admission gates (standing, unchanged)}

\begin{description}[leftmargin=1.9em, itemsep=3pt]
\item[G0 --- Data \& instrument.] Listed ordinary shares; $\geq 2$ years of
daily history spanning at least one stress episode; 5-minute bars for fill
verification. No exceptions: unverifiable means untradeable.
\item[G1 --- Classification.] AI beta and book correlation measured
$\Rightarrow$ AI-related or uncorrelated. Sets the return gate.
\item[G2 --- Return gate.] Planning figure $\geq 50\%$/yr (AI-related) or
$\geq 30\%$/yr (uncorrelated), on verified fills, planning basis.
\item[G3 --- Transportability.] Single split at the half-sample: fit on the
first half only, freeze, score the second. The tested half must support the
plan; where train-only fits collapse far below the observed range, plan on the
low end.
\item[G4 --- Both-halves rule.] Any structural rule counted toward the plan
must improve both halves independently. One regime's evidence is not evidence.
\item[G5 --- Marginal book test.] At equal weight the name must improve the
book it joins: higher planned return, or lower beta / drawdown / stress loss.
A name that neither out-earns nor de-risks re-concentrates. Run with the live
rules on, in both directions.
\end{description}

Your three months cover G0--G3 now (that is the freeze) and the accumulating
forward record; G4/G5 are re-run at review time on the live-rule book.

\section{Sourcing the shortlist}

\begin{checklist}
\item Start from G0, not from conviction: two clean years of daily data
      spanning a stress episode, and 5-minute bars exportable in the standard
      format. Anything that fails this is out before analysis starts.
\item Screen for the fuel: average daily range $\gtrsim 2.5\%$ at workable
      liquidity (the book fills $\sim$500 orders a year without moving
      anything).
\item Aim the mix at the book's need: two or three genuinely uncorrelated
      candidates (AI $\beta \lesssim 0.3$) are worth more than five more AI
      names --- but be honest at G1; the August round reclassified three
      ``diversifiers'' as AI-correlated once measured.
\item Earnings behaviour is part of the record from day one: log each report
      date; report-adjacent entries are where the book's stop-outs cluster.
\end{checklist}

\section{The routine}

\begin{description}[leftmargin=1.9em, itemsep=3pt]
\item[Now (weeks 1--3).] Shortlist $\to$ export data $\to$ G0/G1 $\to$ honest
half-sample fits and the G2/G3 verdicts $\to$ \textbf{freeze} the reference
vectors and open the register: one page per name recording admission date,
classification and gate bar, the frozen vector, the fit evidence
(train / tested / full), and the report-date calendar.
\item[Every Sunday.] Run each frozen vector over the week's archived 5-minute
data; append the would-be fills, exits and stops to the candidate's paper
track. Archive the week's bars (regular hours \emph{and} pre-market) in the
standard folders --- the archive is the evidence; it only exists if it is
kept.
\item[Monthly.] One short note per name: paper-track cumulative vs its gate
bar, anything structural observed (an earnings blow-up, a data problem).
No verdicts --- the review gives verdicts.
\item[December review.] Present the register: one quarter of forward track
per name, G5 re-run on the live-rule book both directions, and an honest
recommendation per name --- \emph{graduate / hold for the second quarter /
drop}.
\end{description}

\section{What kills watch-list work (learned the expensive way)}

\begin{checklist}
\item \textbf{Re-fitting} --- the one sin. See the box in \S1.
\item \textbf{Single-episode evidence.} A rule or a name whose entire case is
      one event in one half (the book's ledger holds several of these
      mirages) fails G4 by construction. If the benefit disappears when you
      remove one week, you have found one week, not a rule.
\item \textbf{Inverted halves.} A tempting full-sample number whose train and
      test halves disagree is the signature of a name that only worked in one
      regime. SMCI and FCX both died of this.
\item \textbf{Quiet improvement of the record.} A missed data day, a
      corrected fill, a re-run --- all fine, all logged. An unlogged change
      to a paper track makes the whole register worthless.
\end{checklist}
