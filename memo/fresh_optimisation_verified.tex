\section*{Setup}

Starting from the top, one blade, no layers. Basis: the live five-name workbook
(engine mirror-checked to the digit on all five Model sheets --- annual return, buys
and stops all reproduce), residual OU sigma, and \textbf{verified same-day fills}: a
same-day round trip is kept only where the 5-minute bars (587 of 591 sessions
covered; the last four fall back to the provable at-open case) show the low reached
the bid before the high reached the target. Protocol: fit on the first half only,
boundary 2025-05-23, freeze, score the second half; the deployed configuration is
scored frozen on the same slice.

Both searches are smaller and better-posed than the old 10-dimensional one:

\textbf{Variant A (8-dim).} $\lambda$ fixed at 1: the model is \emph{exactly}
invariant under $(\lambda,\varphi_L,\psi,k) \to (c\lambda,c\varphi_L,c\psi,k/c)$ ---
confirmed numerically to the sixth decimal --- so the old space carried a pure flat
direction. ou\_W frozen at deployed, the one parameter that held still between
halves. Searched: $\varphi_L$ (log scale), $\psi$, $k$, premium, peak cap, and the
three OU policy knobs.

\textbf{Variant B (6-dim).} The filter $(\lambda,\varphi_L,\psi)$ pinned at its
maximum-likelihood estimate on the train half only (prices identify it; P\&L does
not), ou\_W frozen; only the six policy knobs searched.

Objective: the established robust one ($0.5\cdot$base $+\,0.5\cdot$mean of $\pm3\%$
policy perturbations, minimum-trade floor), computed on the verified train return.

Book, held construction (equal capital, each name compounding independently):
\textbf{84.8\%/yr} over the full sample; 119.6\%/yr over the tested half alone
(the second half was the AI bull run).

The fitted-to-tested collapses are not subtle: RKLB's fresh 8-dim fit turns 289\%
on train into 12\% tested; VST's turns 100\% into 25\%. Removing the exact
degeneracy, freezing ou\_W, pinning the filter by likelihood, and computing the
objective on verified fills --- every methodological improvement available --- did
not change the outcome by one name.

\section*{Reading it honestly}

The deployed configuration was fitted on the full sample, so its tested-half score
contains lookahead; its margin over A and B is \emph{not} evidence that deployed is
optimal. The operative finding is the other direction: \textbf{a fresh parameter
search, on the honest objective, in well-posed reduced spaces, does not transport
out of sample on a single name.} Combined with the rolling-MLE result (the price
process is homogeneous across the sample --- there is no drift for refitting to
track) and the 40-fold historical record, three independent lines of evidence now
say the same thing.

\section*{The clear position}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Plan on the verified numbers}: name-level 50--65\% (RKLB 161\%),
        book $\approx$ 85\%/yr before any out-of-sample haircut. The sheet's cells
        overstate by roughly $3\times$ and should not be quoted.
  \item \textbf{Keep the deployed parameter vector. Stop searching for a better
        one.} The expected gain from any further parameter optimisation is
        approximately zero and the downside (a VST- or RKLB-style mirage going
        live) is real.
  \item \textbf{Future effort goes to structure, not parameters} --- specification
        changes are the only category that has ever survived this blade (residual
        sigma $+7$ to $+13$pp; the 50/50 split). Candidates: sizing or buffers
        conditioned on the OU residual-sigma level, and the still-unimplemented
        GM/VLO/CF diversification.
\end{enumerate}
