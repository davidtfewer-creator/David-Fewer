\section*{\color{bcnavy}Executive summary}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Executive summary}

\keybox{\textbf{The recommendation is to migrate from five names to nine, and the honest
description of that change is a purchase, not an improvement.} The deployed five plan at
\textbf{73.6\%} a year; the nine-name book plans at \textbf{59.1\%}. In exchange, average
pairwise correlation falls from \textbf{0.489 to 0.339}, beta to the AI theme from
\textbf{0.603 to 0.489}, and the loss in the sample's deepest theme drawdown from
\textbf{$-$39.0\% to $-$30.8\%}. That is roughly \textbf{14.5 percentage points of planned
return} for about a fifth less exposure to a single trade, spread across nine legs instead of
five. It should not be presented as a way to earn more.}

Three findings support the change beyond the correlation arithmetic.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em,itemsep=4pt,topsep=4pt]
  \item \textbf{The five-name plan rests on one name.} RKLB contributes 29.2 of the 73.6
        percentage points. Were RKLB merely to match the other four, the five-name plan would
        fall to 55.5\%; were it to earn nothing, to 44.4\%. The nine-name plan is lower but
        does not depend on any single position.
  \item \textbf{The strategy loses to simply holding the stock on the incumbents and beats it
        on the additions.} Over this sample the rule underperforms buy-and-hold on four of five
        AI names and outperforms on all three diversifiers, by 17 to 36 points. The names on
        which the machinery genuinely earns its keep are the ones being added.
  \item \textbf{There is a live test of the insurance, contrary to the record.} A $-$27.9\%
        theme drawdown falls in the tested half of the sample, and the wider books lost 8 to 11
        points less in it.
\end{enumerate}

% ============================ 1 ============================
\section{The proposal}

The deployed book is five names --- RKLB, TSM, VST, VRT and MU --- each taking a fifth of
capital and running two sleeves, Bayesian and Ornstein--Uhlenbeck, at 50/50. The proposal
widens it to nine at 11.1\% each:

\begin{table}[H]\centering\small
\begin{tabular}{llp{0.46\textwidth}}
\toprule
\textbf{Action} & \textbf{Names} & \textbf{Rule} \\
\midrule
Retain & RKLB, TSM, VST, VRT, MU & Deployed rule and parameters, unchanged \\
Add & NVDA, AVGO & Premium 4--6\%, 200-day stop, residual OU sigma with the
                   buffer re-expressed in residual units \\
Add & GM, CF & Diversifier vectors as fitted, unchanged \\
Retire & VLO & Excluded --- see \S7 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

Capital is allocated once and each name compounds independently thereafter --- the \emph{held}
construction. Rebalancing between names is not merely avoided but forbidden: it feeds capital
into falling names and sells winners, and when it was applied inadvertently in earlier work it
cost 16.3 points of return and inflated measured drawdown from 14.9\% to 29.7\%.

% ============================ 2 ============================
\section{Basis of measurement}

Every figure in this document is computed on \textbf{verified fills}: a same-day round trip is
booked only where 5-minute bars prove the low preceded the high. This matters more than any
other methodological choice. The live workbook's own cells report 221\% for VST and 438\% for
RKLB; essentially the entire distance between those numbers and the ones below is same-day fill
verification rather than any disagreement about the model.

Three respects in which this supersedes earlier drafts of the same analysis:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em,itemsep=4pt,topsep=4pt]
  \item \textbf{Nothing is quoted.} Every row is recomputed here from validated workbooks and
        5-minute files on one basis. The engine reproduces each workbook's cached results to the
        penny before any figure is trusted.
  \item \textbf{The at-open floor is no longer used to rank.} It is a hard lower bound but a
        poor ordering device: measured against verified fills its error runs from $+$10.1 points
        (MU) to $+$88.3 points (RKLB), and it reverses the order of TSM and VRT. The original
        case for dropping VST rested on it and did not survive.
  \item \textbf{Planning figures use each name's own measured haircut.} The deployed names carry
        the 2.1-point out-of-sample allowance measured for them; GM, VLO and CF carry
        unseen-window figures, whose measured give-back is far larger. Applying 2.1 points to
        those three, as an earlier draft did, overstated each by roughly 20 points.
\end{enumerate}

Migration costs \textbf{14.5 points} of planned return. Trade activity rises from 419 to 540
buys a year, and drawdown falls from 39.3\% to 30.8\%.

\subsection{Why the return falls, and why that is not a verdict on the added names}

RKLB plans at 146\%. Every other name in the universe plans below 60\%. Under equal weighting
the book return sits close to the average of its names, so \emph{any} addition below the book
average dilutes it, however good the added name is. The five-name book's 73.6\% is not a
property of the strategy; it is a property of holding RKLB at a fifth of capital.

The same arithmetic is the strongest argument for widening. RKLB contributes 29.2 of the 73.6
points. If RKLB merely matched the other four the five-name plan falls to 55.5\%; if RKLB
earned nothing, to 44.4\%. The nine-name plan of 59.1\% is lower, and it stands on nine legs.

The deployed book is one trade. TSM is semiconductors, VRT data-centre cooling, VST

\notebox{\textbf{A measure we deliberately do not lead with.} $R^2$ to the AI factor barely
moves --- and rises slightly --- while beta falls by a fifth. Widening the book scales total
exposure to the theme \emph{down}; it does not change what the exposure \emph{is}. Beta and the
stress windows are the honest measures here. A document that led with $R^2$, or that claimed the
book had stopped being an AI book, would be overstating the case.}

\keybox{\textbf{A correction to the internal record.} Previous work states that the AI factor
rose in every walk-forward test window, so the sample \emph{prices the premium for diversifying
and never tests the insurance}. That holds for the walk-forward folds. It does \textbf{not} hold
for the half-sample split used throughout this document: June--July 2026 is a $-$27.9\% factor

it. \emph{The caveat is stated rather than buried:} the diversifier vectors are full-sample
fits, so that window lies inside their fitted region too, and this is therefore not clean
out-of-sample evidence. It is like-for-like --- the deployed five are also full-sample fits over
the same window --- and it is the closest thing to a live test of the insurance that the sample
contains.}

\section{An inversion worth dwelling on}

The most instructive single result in this analysis is not a book statistic but a comparison
against doing nothing clever at all.

\begin{table}[H]\centering\small
\begin{tabular}{lrrl}
\toprule
\textbf{Name} & \textbf{Strategy} & \textbf{Buy and hold} & \textbf{Does the rule add value?} \\
\midrule
RKLB & 148.1\% & 238.1\% & no, $-$90 points \\
MU   &  61.5\% & 125.2\% & no, $-$64 \\
VRT  &  56.4\% &  66.0\% & no, $-$10 \\
AVGO &  48.7\% &  57.8\% & no, $-$9 \\
TSM  &  56.0\% &  57.0\% & no, $-$1 \\
\midrule
\rowcolor{bccream} VLO &  64.2\% & 28.0\% & \textbf{yes, $+$36} \\
\rowcolor{bccream} CF  &  46.9\% & 16.2\% & \textbf{yes, $+$31} \\
\rowcolor{bccream} GM  &  50.3\% & 32.5\% & \textbf{yes, $+$18} \\
\rowcolor{bccream} VST &  56.7\% & 39.6\% & \textbf{yes, $+$17} \\
\rowcolor{bccream} NVDA&  45.2\% & 42.4\% & \textbf{yes, $+$3} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

The limit-order book \emph{loses} to simply holding the stock on four of five AI names and
\emph{beats} it on all three diversifiers. That is precisely what a dip-buying,
premium-selling rule should do: it forgoes upside in a runaway trend and earns in a choppy or
range-bound one. Regime analysis says the same from the other direction --- the deployed rule's
exposure-matched edge is $+$14.1\% when the theme is weak and $-$17.3\% when it is running.

The implication is uncomfortable for the incumbent book and favourable to migration. Over this
sample the deployed five have been a leveraged bet on the AI theme executed through machinery
that performed \emph{worse} than owning the theme outright. The names on which the machinery
demonstrably earns its keep are the ones proposed for addition.

% ============================ 7 ============================
\section{The two disputed names}

\subsection{VLO --- retire}

VLO returns 64.2\% on halves of 11.4\% and 137.9\%. That spread is the classic
wide-fold overfitting tell, and its measured out-of-sample give-back of 29 points is the largest
of any name in the universe: a full fit of 64.2\% against an unseen-window figure of 35\%.
Excluding it raises the planned book from 56.7\% to 59.1\% and costs 0.040 of correlation. The
trade is worth making.

\subsection{VST --- retain, and a correction}

VST was previously proposed for removal on the observation that it was the weakest of the
deployed five at the at-open floor. \textbf{That basis is disqualified} and the recommendation
is reversed here.

On verified fills VST is indeed the lowest of the deployed five at 56.7\% --- but only barely,
against TSM at 56.0\% and VRT at 56.4\%, a gap well inside the noise of this study. On the
measure that matters for reliability it is \emph{second of ten}: its worst half is 53.4\%,
behind only RKLB. Its two halves are 53.4\% and 59.9\%, against VRT's $-$5.2\% and 152.2\% for
an almost identical average. VST is the steadiest earner in the deployed book, not the weakest
one, and it is among the five names on which the strategy beats buy-and-hold.

Removing it would buy 0.6 of a point of planned return and 0.017 of correlation while deleting the
book's most reliable non-RKLB position. It is retained.

% ============================ 8 ============================
\section{The case against migration}

A justification that argues only one side is not a justification. The strongest objections:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em,itemsep=5pt,topsep=4pt]
  \item \textbf{14.5 points is a large and certain price for an uncertain benefit.} The return
        cost is arithmetic and will be paid in full. The diversification benefit is a claim
        about states of the world that have occurred three times in 2.3 years.
  \item \textbf{Two of the three stress windows lie in the fitted half}, and the third sits
        inside the diversifiers' own fitted region.
  \item \textbf{The added names give back heavily out of sample where it has been measured.}
        GM's full fit of 50.3\% becomes 33\% on unseen windows. The honest expectation for the
        diversifiers is between a half and two-thirds of what the backtest shows, which is why
        their planning figures are set where they are.
  \item \textbf{NVDA and AVGO carry an unmeasured haircut.} Their planning figures apply the
        deployed names' 2.1-point allowance, but nothing establishes a haircut at the
        4--6\%/200-day setting. These are the two softest numbers in the document.
  \item \textbf{Two and a third years is a short sample} containing one broad market regime,
        and the parameters of every name were fitted within it.
\end{enumerate}

% ============================ 9 ============================
\section{Recommendation}

\keybox{\textbf{Migrate to nine names at 11.1\% of capital each: RKLB, TSM, VST, VRT, MU,
NVDA, AVGO, GM and CF.} Planned return 59.1\% on approximately 540 buys a year, maximum
drawdown 30.8\%, average pairwise correlation 0.339 and beta to the AI factor 0.489. Retire
VLO. Run NVDA and AVGO at a 4--6\% premium with a 200-day stop on residual OU sigma, with
buffers re-expressed in residual units --- NVDA 2.2331, AVGO 0.6929.}

\subsection{What would change this recommendation}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em,itemsep=3pt,topsep=4pt]
  \item A \textbf{reversed walk-forward} --- fitting on the late sample and scoring the
        January--April 2025 crash --- would put the insurance genuinely out of sample. It
        remains the single most valuable outstanding test.
  \item A \textbf{measured haircut for NVDA and AVGO} at the patient setting, which would firm
        up the two softest rows in the book.
  \item \textbf{Evidence that RKLB's 146\% is not repeatable} would strengthen the case for
        migration sharply, since the five-name plan depends on it.
\end{itemize}

\notebox{\textbf{What this document does not establish.} That the nine-name book will return
59.1\%. Planning figures are haircut backtests over 2.3 years of a single market regime, and the
interval around every number here is far wider than the differences being discussed. The
defensible claim is comparative --- that the wider book carries roughly a fifth less beta and a
third less average correlation to a single theme, for roughly 14.5 points of expected return ---
not that either level will be realised.}
